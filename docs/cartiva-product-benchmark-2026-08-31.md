# Cartiva product and UX benchmark

Date: 2026-08-31
Scope: current public product surfaces and official help/documentation available without creating accounts or placing orders.

## Reading the findings

- **Observed** describes what the current public experience or official documentation shows.
- **Hypothesis** describes a likely product effect. It is not a conversion claim and should be tested with Cartiva users and privacy-conscious funnel data.
- Account-only and checkout-only states were assessed from official documentation when public interaction was unavailable.

## Comparison matrix

### 1. Walmart

- **A — First impression:** Search and fulfillment context are immediate, while promotions and departments create several competing paths.
- **B — Entry friction:** Public search starts without an account; precise inventory and fulfillment depend on a selected store or delivery address.
- **C — Primary action:** Search is dominant at entry and Add is dominant on product cards; supporting actions are smaller.
- **D — Information hierarchy:** Image, current price, unit price, product name, rating, package, stock, and fulfillment appear before detailed specifications.
- **E — Retention:** Reorder, Purchase History, My Items, lists, recently ordered products, subscriptions, and Store Mode support repeat routines.
- **F — Trust:** Unit and previous pricing, sponsored labels, stock, SNAP eligibility, fulfillment timing, and substitution/final-total explanations are explicit.
- **G — Error recovery:** Shoppers can approve or reject substitutions, edit eligible orders, and return through purchase history without rebuilding the cart.
- **H — Mobile:** Store Mode adds in-store search, maps, aisle location, scanning, list access, and pickup arrival actions.
- **I — Visual discipline:** Reusable cards and familiar controls are consistent, although promotional density creates visual competition.
- **J — Speed perception:** Search reacts quickly and pickup status uses clear progress steps.
- **Hypothesis for Cartiva:** Persistent store context and a recent/reuse path likely reduce repeat-work friction.
- **Sources:** [Walmart home](https://www.walmart.com/), [search](https://www.walmart.com/search?q=milk), [reorder](https://www.walmart.com/help/article/reorder/8c2a6854a3e9428faa93836347c46dd2), [substitutions](https://www.walmart.com/help/article/substitutions-for-store-pickup-and-delivery-items/c8dd3973509b42488da66a362af4666d), [Store Mode](https://www.walmart.com/cp/store-mode/8459006).

### 2. Target

- **A — First impression:** Search, categories, deals, pickup, and delivery are visible immediately; the retail purpose is clear despite multiple paths.
- **B — Entry friction:** Public search is immediate; local availability requires My Store, while Drive Up requires the app and account.
- **C — Primary action:** Add to cart is the strongest product-card action; fulfillment choices are clearly grouped.
- **D — Information hierarchy:** Product, price, package, rating, availability, and fulfillment lead; specifications and policy detail disclose later.
- **E — Retention:** Buy Again, Lists, favorites, Weekly Ad, Circle, Wallet, and remembered substitution preferences support return use.
- **F — Trust:** Selected-store inventory, stock caveats, UPCs, returns, substitutions, and final-total differences are explained near the affected workflow.
- **G — Error recovery:** Preferred substitutions and purchase-history controls let shoppers correct problems without restarting.
- **H — Mobile:** The app combines lists, Buy Again, aisle location, pickup, wallet, and barcode features around large touch controls.
- **I — Visual discipline:** White surfaces, restrained red emphasis, clear headings, and compact cards produce a calm hierarchy.
- **J — Speed perception:** Familiar card actions and staged fulfillment states create immediate feedback.
- **Hypothesis for Cartiva:** Calm surfaces and fulfillment grouping likely make a sophisticated workflow feel familiar.
- **Sources:** [Target home](https://www.target.com/), [search](https://www.target.com/s/fresh%2Bmilk), [Target app](https://www.target.com/c/target-app/-/N-4th2r), [Drive Up and pickup](https://www.target.com/help/articles/delivery-options/drive-up-order-pickup), [substitutions](https://www.target.com/help/article/000150012).

### 4. Instacart

## Eight transferable product principles

1. **Let the shopper begin immediately.** List entry must be local and instant; retailer work begins only after Compare.
2. **Use one outcome action per state.** Add groceries, answer one quick choice, compare the basket, add all items, then review the retailer cart.
3. **Organize complexity behind progressive disclosure.** Show requested item, match, package, price, and status first; keep confidence and evidence under Match details.
4. **Keep store and fulfillment context persistent.** Use one selected location through search, pricing, evidence, basket construction, and handoff.
5. **Compare only complete baskets.** Never let an incomplete basket look cheaper; state why it is excluded and what the shopper can do.
6. **Recover in place and preserve progress.** Clarifications, unmatched items, retries, and OAuth should resume the existing list rather than reset it.
7. **Put evidence where it affects a decision.** Label source, observation time, uncertainty, and checkout boundary without repeating legal copy on every card.
8. **Remember useful shopper state.** Saved lists, historical baskets, price observations, and recent activity should shorten the next visit without turning Cartiva into a dashboard.

## Cartiva audit and disposition

### P0 — primary workflow blockers

- **Observed:** An exact Kroger 12-pack candidate could be rejected when its title omitted “12 pack,” even though Kroger's structured package data reported `12 × 12 fl oz`. This blocked the required five-item basket total and therefore Kroger handoff.
- **Disposition:** Fixed by allowing exact structured pack/count metadata to satisfy the constraint while retaining the contradictory-pack rejection test.

### P1 — major confusion or distrust

- **Observed:** Compare was disabled until the shopper separately pressed Find stores, although the comparison flow already knew how to choose a nearby returned store from a valid ZIP.
- **Disposition:** Fixed. A valid ZIP and resolved grocery choices can now begin comparison; Cartiva selects a nearby returned store and keeps it visible.
- **Observed:** An incomplete result explained the missing match but did not provide a direct recovery action.
- **Disposition:** Fixed with a warm non-error state and an action-oriented Review item control that returns focus to that exact list row.
- **Observed:** Confidence and request/source text appeared on every matched row, making routine results look technical.
- **Disposition:** Fixed. Package, quantity, price, and availability stay visible; request, confidence, and source move under Match details.

### P2 — friction or visual noise

- **Observed:** The list input was visually clear, but the distinction between instant list entry and later retailer work was implicit.
- **Disposition:** Fixed with one concise helper and an empty-state explanation; no network call occurs per keystroke.
- **Observed:** The required funnel had no privacy-conscious event contract.
- **Disposition:** Added a coarse, bounded, transport-free event taxonomy. It excludes grocery text, ZIPs, store IDs, UPCs, and account information. A consent-aware adapter may subscribe later.
- **Observed:** Repeat-use utilities already exist in a quiet desktop rail and move into navigation at narrower widths.
- **Disposition:** Preserved rather than redesigned.

### P3 — optional polish

- **Observed:** Estimated savings used a dash even though only one live retailer is connected, which could read as missing data rather than an unavailable comparison.
- **Disposition:** Clarified that two or more complete retailer baskets are required.
- **Observed:** Store selection controls used terse “Find”/“Change” labels.
- **Disposition:** Refined to “Find stores”/“Change store.”

## Validation hypotheses

The following are hypotheses, not demonstrated conversion effects:

- A valid-ZIP direct Compare path may reduce first-run abandonment.
- Collapsing confidence/evidence may improve scan speed without reducing trust.
- Review item may improve recovery from incomplete baskets.
- The utility rail may shorten repeat comparisons for returning shoppers.
- Clear “two complete baskets” savings language may reduce incorrect savings assumptions.

Validate these with the privacy-conscious funnel, task success, completion time, abandonment point, and qualitative usability sessions. Do not log shopper-entered groceries or precise location data.
